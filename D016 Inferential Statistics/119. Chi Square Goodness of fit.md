# Case Study: Chi-Square Test for Weight Distribution

In this guide, we apply the **Chi-Square Goodness of Fit** test to determine if the weight distribution of a city has changed significantly between 2010 and 2020. This is a classic example of using a sample to make an inference about a shifting population.

---

## 1. The Data and Setup

### 2010 Census (The Theory/Expectation)

The 2010 data provides our "expected" percentages for the population:

* **Less than 50kg:** 20%
* **50 to 75kg:** 30%
* **Greater than 75kg:** 50%

### 2020 Sample (The Observation)

In 2020, we sampled **n = 500** individuals with the following results:

* **Less than 50kg:** 140 people
* **50 to 75kg:** 160 people
* **Greater than 75kg:** 200 people

---

## 2. Converting Percentages to Expected Values (E)

To compare observed counts with the theory, we must convert the 2010 percentages into counts based on the 2020 sample size of 500.

| Weight Category | 2010 Theory (%) | Expected Count (E) | Observed Count (O) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **< 50kg** | 20% | 0.20 * 500 = **100** | 140 |
| **50 to 75kg** | 30% | 0.30 * 500 = **150** | 160 |
| **> 75kg** | 50% | 0.50 * 500 = **250** | 200 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **500** | **500** |

---

## 3. Hypothesis Testing Steps

### Step 1: Define Hypotheses

* **Null Hypothesis (H0):** The 2020 data meets the 2010 expectations (No change in weight distribution).
* **Alternate Hypothesis (H1):** The 2020 data does not meet the 2010 expectations (The distribution has changed).

### Step 2: Significance Level and Degrees of Freedom

* **Alpha (a):** 0.05 (95% Confidence)
* **Degrees of Freedom (df):** Number of categories - 1
* df = 3 - 1 = 2



### Step 3: Find the Critical Value

Using a **Chi-Square Table**, we look up the intersection of **df = 2** and **a = 0.05**.

* **Critical Value:** **5.991**

### Step 4: Decision Boundary

The Chi-Square distribution is not symmetrical; it is skewed to the right.

* If our calculated **X^2** is **> 5.991**, we **Reject H0**.
* If our calculated **X^2** is **< 5.991**, we **Fail to Reject H0**.

---

## 4. Calculating the Chi-Square Statistic

The formula is the sum of (O - E) squared / E for all categories:

1. **Category 1 (< 50kg):** (140 - 100) squared / 100 = 1600 / 100 = **16**
2. **Category 2 (50-75kg):** (160 - 150) squared / 150 = 100 / 150 = **0.66**
3. **Category 3 (> 75kg):** (200 - 250) squared / 250 = 2500 / 250 = **10**

**Total Chi-Square (X^2) = 16 + 0.66 + 10 = 26.66**

---

## 5. Final Conclusion

* **Calculated X^2 (26.66) > Critical Value (5.991)**

Because our test statistic falls deep into the rejection region, we **Reject the Null Hypothesis**.

**The Result:** We conclude with 95% confidence that the weight distribution of the population in 2020 is significantly different from the distribution recorded in 2010.

---

## Key Terminology

* **Degrees of Freedom (df):** In a goodness of fit test, this is always the number of categories minus one.
* **Observed (O):** The actual frequencies obtained from the sample.
* **Expected (E):** The frequencies we would expect if the null hypothesis were true.
* **Chi-Square Table:** A reference table used to find the critical value required to reject the null hypothesis.
