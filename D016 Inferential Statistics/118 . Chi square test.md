# Chi-Square Test: Goodness of Fit

The **Chi-Square Test** is a powerful statistical method used to determine if there is a significant difference between the **observed frequencies** and the **expected frequencies** in one or more categories. It is specifically designed for analyzing **categorical data**.

---

## 1. Core Characteristics

The Chi-Square (represented as **X^2**) test for **Goodness of Fit** is unique because it claims something about the **population proportion**.

* **Non-Parametric Test:** It does not assume that the data follows a normal distribution.
* **Data Type:** It is performed on categorical data, which includes:
* **Nominal Data:** Categories with no inherent order (e.g., colors, gender).
* **Ordinal Data:** Categories with a set order (e.g., "Satisfied," "Neutral," "Dissatisfied").


* **Purpose:** To see how well a sample distribution fits a theoretical distribution (the "theory").

---

## 2. Theory vs. Observed Distributions

To conduct this test, we compare two distinct distributions:

| Distribution Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Theory (Expected)** | The distribution we expect to see based on a theory or a known population proportion. |
| **Observed** | The actual data collected from our sample or experiment. |

### The Goal of Goodness of Fit

We use the Chi-Square test to decide: **Does the observed sample information support the theory?** If the difference between what we observe and what we expect is very small, we say it is a "good fit."

---

## 3. Practical Example: Science Class Left-Handedness

Let's break down the logic using the science class scenario.

**The Theory:** 12% of people in the general population are left-handed.
**The Sample:** A class of 75 students has 11 left-handed students.

### Step 1: Define Categories

Our categories are **Left-Handed** and **Right-Handed**.

### Step 2: Create the Observed Table

Based on the sample of 75 students:

* **Left-Handed:** 11
* **Right-Handed:** 64 (Total 75 - 11)

### Step 3: Calculate the Expected Table

Based on the theory (12%):

* **Expected Left:** 12% of 75 = (12/100) * 75 = **9 students**
* **Expected Right:** 88% of 75 = (88/100) * 75 = **66 students**

### Step 4: Compare

We now have two sets of values for the same group of 75 people:

| Category | Observed (O) | Expected (E) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Left-Handed** | 11 | 9 |
| **Right-Handed** | 64 | 66 |

---

## 4. The Chi-Square Formula

While we will solve the full math in the next session, the formula used to calculate the Chi-Square statistic is:

**X^2 = sum of [(O - E)^2 / E]**

Where:

* **O** = Observed Frequency
* **E** = Expected Frequency

---

## Key Terminology

* **Categorical Variable:** A variable that can take on one of a limited, and usually fixed, number of possible values (e.g., Bike Color).
* **Population Proportion:** The percentage of a specific attribute found in the entire population.
* **Non-Parametric:** Statistics that do not rely on assumptions about the shape or parameters of the underlying population distribution.
