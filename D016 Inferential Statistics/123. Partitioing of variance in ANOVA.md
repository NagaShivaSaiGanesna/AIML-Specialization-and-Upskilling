# Hypothesis Testing in ANOVA: Partitioning of Variance

In this guide, we walk through the step-by-step process of performing hypothesis testing using **ANOVA**. The core logic involves partitioning the total variance in a dataset into two components: variance **between** groups and variance **within** groups.

---

## 1. The Statistical Hypotheses

Unlike a T-test where we compare two means, ANOVA tests multiple means simultaneously.

* **Null Hypothesis (H0):** All group means are equal. (mu1 = mu2 = mu3 = ... = muk)
* **Alternate Hypothesis (H1):** At least one of the sample means is significantly different from the others.
* **Note:** We do not say "all means are unequal," only that at least one pair is different.



---

## 2. The Test Statistic: F-Test

The test statistic used in ANOVA is the **F-statistic**. It is a ratio of two different estimates of variance:

**F = Variance Between Samples / Variance Within Samples**

### Breaking Down the Variance

1. **Variance Between Samples:** Measures how much the means of the different groups vary from each other.
2. **Variance Within Samples:** Measures the spread of the data points inside each individual group.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step ANOVA Case Study

**Scenario:** A doctor tests three dosages of a headache medication: **15mg**, **30mg**, and **45mg**. Patients rate their headache relief from 1 to 10.

### Step 1: Define Hypotheses

* **H0:** mu15 = mu30 = mu45
* **H1:** At least one mean is different.

### Step 2: Set Significance and Degrees of Freedom (df)

* **Alpha:** 0.05
* **df_between:** (Number of categories - 1) = 3 - 1 = **2**
* **df_within:** (Total observations - Categories) = 21 - 3 = **18**
* **df_total:** (Total observations - 1) = 21 - 1 = **20**

### Step 3: Identify Critical Value

Using an **F-table** with df1 = 2 and df2 = 18 at Alpha = 0.05:

* **Critical F:** **3.5546**

### Step 4: Calculate Sum of Squares (SS) and Mean Squares (MS)

This is the "heavy lifting" phase where we calculate the Sum of Squares for both components.

| Source of Variation | Sum of Squares (SS) | df | Mean Square (MS) | F-Statistic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Between Groups** | 98.67 | 2 | 49.34 (98.67 / 2) | **86.56** |
| **Within Groups** | 10.29 | 18 | 0.57 (10.29 / 18) |  |
| **Total** | 108.96 | 20 |  |  |

---

## 4. The Decision Rule

Our calculated F-statistic is **86.56**.

* **Decision:** Since **86.56 > 3.5546**, we fall deep into the rejection region.
* **Result:** We **Reject the Null Hypothesis**.

**Conclusion:** There is a statistically significant difference in headache relief between the three medication dosages.

---

## ANOVA Summary Table (The "Source Table")

In professional reports and research papers, ANOVA results are always presented in this standardized format:

| Source | SS | df | MS | F |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Between** | SS_between | k - 1 | MS_between | MS_between / MS_within |
| **Within (Error)** | SS_within | N - k | MS_within |  |
| **Total** | SS_total | N - 1 |  |  |

---

## Key Terminology

* **Partitioning:** Dividing the total variation into specific components.
* **Sum of Squares (SS):** The sum of the squared differences between data points and the mean.
* **Mean Square (MS):** The Sum of Squares divided by the degrees of freedom (essentially the variance).
* **F-Table:** A reference table used to find critical values for the F-distribution based on two different degrees of freedom.
