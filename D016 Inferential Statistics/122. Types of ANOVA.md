# Types of ANOVA

In this guide, we break down the three primary types of **Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)**. Choosing the right type depends on the structure of your data—specifically the number of **Factors** (independent variables) and whether the **Levels** (sub-groups) are independent or dependent.

---

## 1. One-Way ANOVA

A **One-Way ANOVA** is the most basic form. It is used when you have exactly **one factor** with at least two levels, and those levels are **independent**.

* **Logic:** You are comparing different, unrelated groups of people or objects.
* **Example:** A doctor tests a new headache medication using three different dosages: **10mg**, **20mg**, and **30mg**.
* **Factor:** Medication.
* **Levels:** 10mg, 20mg, 30mg.
* **Independence:** Each group consists of different participants. The person taking 10mg has no influence on the person taking 30mg.



---

## 2. Repeated Measures ANOVA

A **Repeated Measures ANOVA** is used when you have **one factor**, but the **levels are dependent**. This is often called a "within-subjects" design.

* **Logic:** You are measuring the *same* group of people multiple times under different conditions.
* **Example:** Tracking the performance of runners over three days (**Day 1**, **Day 2**, and **Day 3**).
* **Factor:** Running time/duration.
* **Levels:** Day 1, Day 2, Day 3.
* **Dependence:** The data is dependent because the same individual’s performance on Day 1 likely influences or relates to their performance on Day 2 and Day 3.



---

## 3. Factorial ANOVA

A **Factorial ANOVA** is used when you have **two or more factors**. Each factor must have at least two levels. These levels can be either independent or dependent.

* **Logic:** You want to see how multiple independent variables interact with each other to affect the outcome.
* **Example:** Analyzing running performance based on both **Time** (Day 1, Day 2, Day 3) and **Gender** (Male, Female).
* **Factor 1:** Running Time (3 levels: Day 1, Day 2, Day 3).
* **Factor 2:** Gender (2 levels: Male, Female).


* **Benefit:** This allows you to see if the effect of running time differs specifically between males and females (an "interaction effect").

---

## Comparison Table of ANOVA Types

| ANOVA Type | No. of Factors | Relationship of Levels | Typical Use Case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **One-Way** | 1 | Independent | Comparing different groups (e.g., 3 different diets). |
| **Repeated Measures** | 1 | Dependent | Tracking the same group over time (e.g., pre-test vs. post-test). |
| **Factorial** | 2+ | Independent or Dependent | Complex studies with multiple variables (e.g., Diet and Exercise Type). |

---

## Key Terminology

* **One-Way:** Refers to having a single independent variable.
* **Repeated Measures:** Also known as "Within-Subjects" design.
* **Interaction Effect:** Found in Factorial ANOVA; it occurs when the effect of one factor changes depending on the level of another factor.
* **Factor:** The categorical independent variable.
* **Level:** The specific categories within a factor.
