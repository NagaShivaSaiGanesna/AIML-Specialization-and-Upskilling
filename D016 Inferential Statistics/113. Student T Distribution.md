
# T-Test: Step-by-Step Problem Solving

When we don't know the **population standard deviation (sigma)**, we must use the **sample standard deviation (s)** and the **T-distribution**.

### The Problem Scenario

A drug manufacturer claims a new medication reduces blood pressure by **10 units** (mu). A doctor tests this on **10 patients** (n) and finds a sample mean reduction of **15 units** (x-bar) with a sample standard deviation of **4 units** (s). We will test this at a **95% confidence level**.

### 1. State the Hypotheses

* **Null Hypothesis (H0)**: mu = 10 (The medicine performs as claimed).
* **Alternate Hypothesis (H1)**: mu != 10 (The performance is different).

### 2. Calculate Degrees of Freedom (DOF)

Using the formula **n - 1**:

* DOF = 10 - 1 = **9**.

### 3. Calculate the T-Statistic

We use the formula: **t = (x-bar - mu) / (s / root(n))**.

* **Step A**: Calculate the Standard Error: 4 / root(10) = 4 / 3.16 = **1.26**.
* **Step B**: Calculate T: (15 - 10) / 1.26 = 5 / 1.26 = **3.97**.

### 4. Find the Critical Value (T-Table)

* **Alpha (significance)**: 1 - 0.95 = **0.05**.
* For a **two-tailed test** with **DOF = 9** and **alpha = 0.05**, we look at the T-table.
* The critical value is approximately **2.262**.

### 5. The Conclusion

We compare our calculated **t (3.97)** to the critical value (**2.262**).

| Value Type | Result | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Calculated T** | 3.97 | Is 3.97 > 2.262? |
| **Critical T** | 2.262 | **YES** |

**Final Decision**: We **Reject the Null Hypothesis**. The medicine's effect is significantly different from the 10-unit claim.
