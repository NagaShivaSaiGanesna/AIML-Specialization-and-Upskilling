# P-Value and Significance in Hypothesis Testing

The **P-Value** is a fundamental metric in **inferential statistics**. It represents the probability of observing your sample results (or something more extreme) assuming that the **Null Hypothesis (H0)** is actually true. It serves as the evidence we use to decide whether to stick with our initial assumption or reject it in favor of an alternative.

---

## Intuition: The Spacebar Example

To understand P-value intuitively, imagine the distribution of where you touch the **spacebar** on a keyboard:

* **Central Region**: Most touches happen in the middle. If you touch here, the probability is high (e.g., P-value = 0.8), meaning this is a "normal" or expected event.
* **Edges**: Occasionally, you might touch the very edge. If the P-value at the edge is 0.02, it means that out of 100 touches, you only hit that specific spot about 2 times.
* **In Statistics**: A very low P-value suggests that the observed outcome is rare and unlikely to happen by random chance if the null hypothesis is true.

---
