# Hypothesis Testing

**Hypothesis Testing** is a core pillar of **inferential statistics**. Its primary goal is to use **sample data** to reach conclusions or "inferences" about an unknown **population parameter**, such as a mean, variance, or standard deviation. Because analyzing an entire population is often impossible, we use this structured mechanism to determine if our assumptions about the population hold true.

---

## The Hypothesis Testing Mechanism

Hypothesis testing follows a strict, step-by-step process to ensure objective conclusions.

### 1. Null Hypothesis (H0)

The **Null Hypothesis** is the default assumption you begin with. It represents the status quo or a "no change" scenario.

* **Example**: In a court of law, the default assumption is that the person is **not guilty** until proven otherwise.

### 2. Alternate Hypothesis (H1)

The **Alternate Hypothesis** is the direct opposite of the null hypothesis. It represents the claim or theory that you are trying to prove.

* **Example**: The person is **guilty**.

### 3. Statistical Analysis (Experiments)

To decide between H0 and H1, you must perform **statistical analysis** on your sample data.

* **Methods**: Depending on the data, you might use a **Z-test**, **T-test**, **Chi-square test**, **ANOVA**, or **F-test**.
* **Analogy**: In our court example, this step involves collecting evidence like DNA tests or fingerprints to see if they contradict the null hypothesis.

### 4. Conclusion

Based on the results of the experiment, you either **Accept the Null Hypothesis** (fail to reject it) or **Reject the Null Hypothesis** in favor of the alternate hypothesis.

---

## Practical Example: College Pass Percentages

Imagine a district where the established pass percentage is **85%**. A new college claims its results might be different.

### Defining the Hypotheses

Based on the scenario, we set up our testing framework as follows:

| Hypothesis Type | Notation | Definition in this Case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Null Hypothesis** | **H0** | The average pass percentage is **85%** (mu = 85%). |
| **Alternate Hypothesis** | **H1** | The pass percentage is **different** from 85% (mu ≠ 85%). |

> **Note**: If the question asked if the percentage was "greater than" 85%, the alternate hypothesis would be (mu > 85%). However, because we are testing for "different," we use the "not equal to" sign.
