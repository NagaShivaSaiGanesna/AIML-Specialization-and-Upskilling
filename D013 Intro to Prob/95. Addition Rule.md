# Introduction to Probability and the Addition Rule

In statistics and machine learning, **Probability** is the foundation for decision-making. When a model performs classification, it calculates the probability of an input belonging to a certain class (e.g., Spam vs. Not Spam) and uses a threshold to make the final call.

---

## 1. What is Probability?

Probability is the measure of the **likelihood** that an event or experiment will occur. It is quantified as a number between 0 and 1 (or 0% and 100%).

### Core Examples:

* **Tossing a Coin:** The possible outcomes (Sample Space) are {Head, Tail}.
* Probability of Head: **1/2 (50%)**


* **Rolling a Dice:** The possible outcomes are {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
* Probability of getting a 1: **1/6**
* Probability of getting a 2: **1/6**



---

## 2. Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events are **Mutually Exclusive** if they **cannot occur at the same time**. If one happens, the other cannot.

* **Example:** When tossing a single coin, you cannot get both a Head and a Tail simultaneously.
* **Visualizing:** In a Venn diagram, these events are represented by circles that do not touch or overlap.

### The Addition Rule (Mutually Exclusive)

When events are mutually exclusive, the probability of Event A **OR** Event B occurring is simply the sum of their individual probabilities:

**P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)**

**Example (Rolling a Dice):**
What is the probability of rolling a 1 or a 5?

* P(1) = 1/6
* P(5) = 1/6
* **P(1 or 5)** = 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 = **1/3**

---

## 3. Non-Mutually Exclusive Events

**Non-Mutually Exclusive** events are those that **can occur at the same time**. There is an overlap where both events happen together.

* **Example:** Drawing a card from a deck and getting a **King** OR a **Heart**. You could draw the **King of Hearts**, which satisfies both conditions simultaneously.
* **Visualizing:** In a Venn diagram, these events overlap. The overlapping area represents the intersection (both events happening).

### The Addition Rule (Non-Mutually Exclusive)

To avoid double-counting the overlapping part, we must subtract the intersection:

**P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)**

**Example (Deck of Cards):**
What is the probability of picking a King (K) or a Heart?

1. **P(K):** There are 4 Kings in a deck of 52. -> **4/52**
2. **P(Heart):** There are 13 Hearts in a deck of 52. -> **13/52**
3. **P(K and Heart):** There is only 1 King of Hearts. -> **1/52**

**Calculation:**
P(K or Heart) = (4/52) + (13/52) - (1/52)
P(K or Heart) = 17/52 - 1/52 = **16/52**

---

## Summary Table: Addition Rules

| Event Type | Definition | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mutually Exclusive** | Cannot happen at the same time | **P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)** |
| **Non-Mutually Exclusive** | Can happen at the same time | **P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)** |
