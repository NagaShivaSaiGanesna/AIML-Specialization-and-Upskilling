# Multiplication Rule: Independent and Dependent Events

In our continuation of probability, we move from the **Addition Rule** (used for "OR" scenarios) to the **Multiplication Rule** (used for "AND" scenarios). This allows us to calculate the probability of multiple events occurring in sequence.

---

## 1. Independent Events

Two events are **Independent** if the occurrence of one does not change the probability of the other. In other words, they do not affect each other.

* **Example 1: Tossing a Coin:** If you toss a coin and get a Head, the probability of getting a Tail on the second toss remains exactly 1/2. The first result has no memory or influence over the second.
* **Example 2: Rolling a Dice:** Rolling a 1 on your first turn does not change your 1/6 chance of rolling a 2 on your second turn.

### The Multiplication Rule (Independent)

When events are independent, the probability of both Event A **AND** Event B occurring is the product of their individual probabilities:

**P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B)**

**Example (Tossing a Coin twice):**
What is the probability of getting a Head and then a Tail?

* P(Head) = 1/2
* P(Tail) = 1/2
* **P(Head and Tail)** = (1/2) * (1/2) = **1/4**

---

## 2. Dependent Events

Two events are **Dependent** if the outcome of the first event affects the probability of the second event.

* **Example: Drawing Cards without Replacement:** Imagine drawing a card from a deck of 52. You draw a **King** and keep it out of the deck. Now, there are only 51 cards left. If you try to draw a **Queen** next, the sample space has changed, making this a dependent event.

### Conditional Probability

This leads to the concept of **Conditional Probability**, which is the probability of an event occurring *given* that another event has already occurred. This is a foundational concept for **Bayes' Theorem** and the **Naive Bayes** machine learning algorithm.

### The Multiplication Rule (Dependent)

To find the probability of two dependent events occurring, we multiply the probability of the first event by the conditional probability of the second:

**P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B|A)**

*(Note: **P(B|A)** is read as "The probability of B given that A has occurred")*

**Example (Deck of Cards):**
What is the probability of drawing a King (K) and then a Queen (Q) without putting the King back?

1. **P(K):** There are 4 Kings in 52 cards. -> **4/52**
2. **P(Q|K):** There are 4 Queens, but only **51** cards left in the deck. -> **4/51**

**Calculation:**
P(K and Q) = (4/52) * (4/51)
**P(K and Q) = 16 / 2652**

### Key Takeaways for Machine Learning:

* **Multiplication Rule** is the "AND" rule.
* **Independent events** are common in simple models (like assuming features don't interact).
* **Dependent events** and **Conditional Probability** are the core of **Bayes' Theorem**, which is used in classification tasks like spam detection.
