# Estimates in Inferential Statistics

Estimates mark the transition from **descriptive statistics** (summarizing data) to **inferential statistics** (making predictions about a larger group). An estimate is a specific numerical value observed in a sample that is used to approximate an unknown **population parameter**.

---

## Types of Estimates

There are two primary ways to estimate a population parameter: by using a single value or by using a range.

### 1. Point Estimate

A **Point Estimate** is a single numerical value used to estimate an unknown population parameter.

* **Intuition**: It provides a "best guess" for the population.
* **Primary Example**: The **sample mean (x-bar)** is the point estimate of the **population mean (mu)**.
* **Limitation**: Because it is just one number, it may differ significantly from the true population value due to sampling variability.

### 2. Interval Estimate

An **Interval Estimate** uses a range of values to estimate an unknown population parameter.

* **Intuition**: Since a single point estimate (like 60) might miss the true population mean (like 65), we provide a buffer or a range.
* **Confidence Interval**: This range is often referred to as a **Confidence Interval**.
* **Formula Concept**: Interval Estimate = Point Estimate +/- Margin of Error.
* **Example**: If your sample mean is 60 but you expect some error, you might provide an interval estimate of **55 to 65** to better represent where the population mean truly lies.

---

## Comparison of Estimates

| Feature | Point Estimate | Interval Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Output** | A single value (e.g., 60) | A range of values (e.g., 55 to 65) |
| **Precision** | Very specific | Less specific but more likely to be "correct" |
| **Reliability** | Lower (sensitive to single-sample errors) | Higher (includes a margin of error) |
| **Components** | Sample Statistic | Point Estimate + Margin of Error |

---

### Why Use Estimates?

In the real world, we rarely have access to the entire population. Estimates allow us to use a small, manageable **sample** to draw meaningful conclusions about the **unknown parameters** of the entire population.
