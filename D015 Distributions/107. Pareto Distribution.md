# Pareto Distribution

The **Pareto Distribution** is a specific type of **power law distribution** that strictly adheres to the **80/20 rule** (the Pareto Principle). It is a **non-Gaussian** (non-normal) distribution used to describe systems where a small number of causes are responsible for a large majority of the effects.

---

## Mathematical Intuition

The shape of a Pareto distribution is defined by its **Probability Density Function (PDF)** and a key hyperparameter known as **alpha**.

### The Role of Alpha

* **Shape Parameter:** The **alpha** value determines the steepness of the curve.
* **Impact of Increasing Alpha:** As the alpha value increases, the height of the PDF increases, and the distribution becomes more "peaked" as it approaches a value of 1 for the random variable **x**.
* **The Long Tail:** Regardless of the alpha value, the distribution maintains a "long tail," representing the many small factors that contribute to the remaining 20% of the total effect.

---

## Real-World Examples in Industry

The Pareto distribution is widely used in the **IT industry** and economics to identify where efforts should be focused for maximum impact.

| Scenario | Pareto Intuition (80/20 Rule) |
| --- | --- |
| **Project Management** | Often, **80% of the project work** is completed by only **20% of the team members**. |
| **Bug Fixing** | Approximately **80% of software defects** can be resolved by identifying and fixing the **20% of "majority" core defects** that cause them. |
| **Wealth and Salary** | In many companies, **80% of the total salary budget** is distributed among the **top 20% of individuals**. |
| **Resource Allocation** | A small percentage (**20%**) of people or assets often hold the vast majority (**80%**) of the weight or value in a system. |

## Data Transformation: Converting to Normal Distribution

Most machine learning algorithms perform better when data follows a **Gaussian (Normal) Distribution**. Because Pareto data is highly skewed, it must be transformed to improve model performance.

### Box-Cox Transformation

While log-normal distributions use simple log normalization, a **Pareto Distribution** requires a **Box-Cox Transformation** to become normal.

* **Purpose:** This mathematical technique reshapes the skewed power-law data into a symmetric bell curve.
* **Why Transform?:** Machine learning models often have underlying assumptions that data is normally distributed; meeting these assumptions increases the model's accuracy.
* **Implementation:** This is commonly performed using Python libraries during the data preprocessing stage.

### Comparison of Transformation Techniques

| Original Distribution | Transformation Technique | Resulting Distribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Log-Normal** | Log Normalization | Gaussian (Normal) |
| **Pareto / Power Law** | **Box-Cox Transformation** | Gaussian (Normal) |
