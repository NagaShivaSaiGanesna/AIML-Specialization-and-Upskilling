# Power Law Distribution

The **Power Law Distribution** is a functional relationship between two quantities where a relative change in one quantity results in a proportional relative change in the other, regardless of the initial size. It is fundamentally different from a **Gaussian (Normal) Distribution** because it does not cluster around a central average; instead, it is characterized by a "long tail" where a few extreme values dominate the system.

---

## The 80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)

The Power Law is most famously known as the **80/20 Rule**. This principle suggests that a large majority of effects (80%) come from a small minority of causes (20%).

### Visualizing the Curve

* **The Head:** The left side of the curve represents the few items that dominate the distribution (the 20%).
* **The Long Tail:** The right side of the curve represents the vast majority of items that have a very small individual impact but collectively make up the rest of the distribution.

### Comparison of Distribution Shapes

| Distribution | Curve Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Normal** | Symmetric bell curve centered around a mean. |
| **Log-Normal** | Right-skewed distribution that becomes normal when logged. |
| **Power Law** | L-shaped curve with a heavy "long tail". |

## Real-World Examples

The 80/20 rule appears across many different domains, from sports to economics and linguistics:

* **Sports (IPL):** Roughly 20% of teams or players may be responsible for winning 80% of the matches or scoring 80% of the runs.
* **Wealth:** 80% of total wealth is often distributed among only 20% of the population.
* **Natural Resources:** Approximately 80% of the world's crude oil is held by about 20% of the nations.
* **Linguistics:** The frequency of words in most languages follows a power law; a few words (like "the", "and") appear very often, while most words appear rarely.
* **Software Development:** 20% of major code defects are often responsible for 80% of the system crashes or upcoming bugs.

## Data Transformation

In data science, many Machine Learning algorithms perform better when the input data follows a **Normal Distribution**. If your data follows a Power Law, you must transform it to make it Gaussian.

### Box-Cox Transformation

While Log-Normal data can be normalized using a simple log transformation, Power Law data typically requires a **Box-Cox Transformation**.

* **Purpose:** To convert a power law or Pareto distribution into a normal distribution.
* **Validation:** After applying the transform, a **Q-Q Plot** is used to validate if the data now follows a normal distribution.

> **Note:** A specific type of power law distribution is the **Pareto Distribution**, which we will cover in the next section to see how it strictly applies the 80/20 rule to data analysis.
