## 1. Bias (The Training Set Problem)

Bias is a measure of how well the model can fit the **Training Data**.

* **Definition:** High Bias occurs when the model is too simple to capture the underlying patterns in your training data.
* **The "Training Data" Sign:** You will see a **high error rate on your Training Set**. Even though the model has the "answer key" (the training labels), it still can't get the answers right because its "logic" is too limited.
* **Common Term:** This is called **Underfitting**.
* **In an Interview:** "If my training error is high, I likely have a High Bias problem. I should consider a more complex model or adding more features."

---

## 2. Variance (The Testing Set Problem)

Variance is a measure of the **Gap** between Training performance and Testing performance.

* **Definition:** High Variance occurs when the model "memorizes" the specific noise and random fluctuations of the training data instead of learning the general rule.
* **The "Testing Data" Sign:** You will see a **low error on Training Data** but a **high error on Testing Data**. The model looks like a genius during practice but fails the final exam.
* **Common Term:** This is called **Overfitting**.
* **In an Interview:** "If my training error is low but my test error is significantly higher, I have a High Variance problem. The model is failing to generalize to unseen data."

---

## 3. The "Gap" Summary Table

This is the most concise way to visualize it for an interviewer:

| Scenario | Training Error | Testing Error | Diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **High Bias** | High | High | **Underfitting:** The model is too simple for the data. |
| **High Variance** | Low | High | **Overfitting:** The model is too sensitive to the training data. |
| **The Sweet Spot** | Low | Low | **Good Generalization:** The model learned the signal, not the noise. |

---

## 4. The Mathematical "Feel"

Think of the total error on your **Test Set** as being composed of two main "debt" collectors:

$$\text{Test Error} = \underbrace{\text{Bias}^2}_{\text{Error from being too simple}} + \underbrace{\text{Variance}}_{\text{Error from being too sensitive}} + \text{Noise}$$

* If you make the model more complex to lower the **Bias**, you pay for it by increasing the **Variance**.
* If you simplify the model to lower the **Variance**, you pay for it by increasing the **Bias**.
