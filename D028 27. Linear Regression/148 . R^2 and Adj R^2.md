# ML Study Guide: Understanding $R^2$ (Coefficient of Determination)

## 1. The Core Definition

$R^2$ is the **percentage of variation** in the dependent variable ($Y$) that is explained by the independent variable ($X$).

* **High $R^2$ (e.g., 0.89):** $X$ does a great job of explaining why $Y$ changes.
* **Low $R^2$ (e.g., 0.01):** $X$ has almost no relationship with $Y$ (e.g., butter consumption vs. murder rates).

---

## 2. The Baseline: "The Naive Alien"

Before we have a model, our best guess for any future data point is simply the **mean (average)** of the data we have already seen.

* **Example Data:** Weights of 100 lb, 150 lb, and 200 lb.
* **Mean ($\bar{y}$):** 150 lb.
* **The "Error":** If we guess 150 lb for everyone, our errors are 50, 0, and 50.
* **SS Total ($SS_{tot}$):** The sum of these squared errors.

$$50^2 + 0^2 + 50^2 = 5,000$$



> **Concept:** $SS_{total}$ represents the total "uncertainty" or variation in the data before we try to explain it with a model.

---

## 3. The Improved Model: "The Regression Line"

Instead of just guessing the mean, we use a relationship (like Height) to predict Weight.

* **The Formula:** $\hat{y} = \text{Intercept} + \text{Slope} \times \text{Height}$
* **Residuals:** The vertical distance between the actual data point and our line's prediction.
* **SS Error ($SS_{res}$):** The sum of these new squared residuals.
* In the video example, using the "Green Line" reduced the error from **5,000** down to **535.7**.



---

## 4. Calculating $R^2$

$R^2$ measures how much of that original 5,000 "error" we managed to get rid of.

### The Logic:

1. **Total Variance ($SS_{tot}$):** 5,000
2. **Unexplained Variance ($SS_{res}$):** 535.7
3. **Explained Variance ($SS_{reg}$):** $5,000 - 535.7 = 4,464.3$

### The Formulas:

$$R^2 = \frac{SS_{reg}}{SS_{tot}} \quad \text{or} \quad R^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_{res}}{SS_{tot}}$$

**In the example:**


$$R^2 = \frac{4,464.3}{5,000} = 0.893 \text{ (or 89.3%)}$$

---

## 5. Key Takeaways for Interviews

* **Least Squares Regression:** This specific method is designed to **minimize** the $SS_{res}$. Because $SS_{res}$ is minimized, $R^2$ is automatically **maximized**.
* **Interpretation:** An $R^2$ of 0.89 means that 89% of the "wiggles" or variations in weight are accounted for by changes in height. The remaining 11% is "noise" or due to other factors (diet, muscle mass, etc.).
* **The Scale:** $R^2$ typically ranges from 0 to 1.
* **0:** The model is no better than just guessing the mean.
* **1:** The model perfectly predicts every single data point.



## 1. The Problem: $R^2$ is Greedy

The biggest secret about $R^2$ is that it **never goes down**.

If you add a new variable to your model—even something completely stupid like "Amount of curd consumed" to predict "Car Price"—your $R^2$ will either stay the same or go up. It will **never** decrease.

* **The Logic:** The math looks at the new variable and thinks, "Even if this curd data is 99% useless, I can probably find 1% of random luck in these numbers to help me fit the training dots a little better."
* **The Result:** You think your model is getting better, but you are actually just adding **noise**. This is a recipe for **High Variance (Overfitting)**.

---

## 2. The Solution: Adjusted $R^2$

Adjusted $R^2$ is the "honest" version of $R^2$. It adds a **penalty** for every new variable you add to the model.

* **If the variable is useful:** The error ($SS_{res}$) drops so much that it overcomes the penalty. Adjusted $R^2$ **goes up**.
* **If the variable is useless:** The penalty is bigger than the tiny improvement in error. Adjusted $R^2$ **goes down**.

---

## 3. The "Alien" Intuition

Imagine the Alien adds a second variable to predict weight: **"Number of teeth."**

1. **With $R^2$:** The model finds a tiny random pattern between teeth and weight. $R^2$ moves from 89.3% to 89.4%. The Alien thinks, "Great! Teeth help!"
2. **With Adjusted $R^2$:** The math says, "Wait, you added a whole new variable for a tiny 0.1% gain? That's not worth the complexity." The Adjusted $R^2$ drops from 88% to 85%.

**The Signal:** The drop in Adjusted $R^2$ tells the Alien to **delete** the "teeth" variable.

---

## 4. The Formula (The Math Behind the Penalty)

You don't need to memorize this for a high-level conversation, but you should understand the components:

$$R^2_{adj} = 1 - \left[ \frac{(1 - R^2)(n - 1)}{n - k - 1} \right]$$

* **$n$:** Number of data points (sample size).
* **$k$:** Number of independent variables (features).

**How it works:**
As $k$ (the number of variables) increases, the denominator $(n - k - 1)$ gets smaller. This makes the "penalty" term larger. To keep the score high, your $R^2$ has to increase significantly to "earn" its place.

---

## 5. Summary for FAANG Interviews

When an interviewer asks, "Why do we use Adjusted $R^2$ instead of $R^2$?"

* **The "Junior" Answer:** "Because $R^2$ always increases when you add features."
* **The "FAANG" Answer:** "Adjusted $R^2$ accounts for **model complexity**. It prevents us from overfitting by penalizing the addition of non-significant predictors. If adding a feature doesn't improve the model more than what would be expected by chance, the Adjusted $R^2$ will decrease, signaling that the feature should be removed."
