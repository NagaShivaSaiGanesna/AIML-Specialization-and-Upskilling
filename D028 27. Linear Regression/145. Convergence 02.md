# Multidimensional Gradient Descent in Simple Linear Regression

In our previous discussions, we simplified the visualization of **Gradient Descent** by assuming the intercept (θ₀) was zero. In a real-world scenario, we must optimize both the **Intercept (θ₀)** and the **Slope (θ₁)** simultaneously to find the absolute best fit line.

---

## 1. Visualizing the 3D Cost Surface

When we have two parameters to optimize (θ₀ and θ₁), the **Cost Function J(θ₀, θ₁)** is no longer a simple 2D curve. It becomes a 3D surface, often resembling an inverted mountain or a "bowl."

* **The Bowl:** Every point on the surface of this bowl represents a specific combination of θ₀ and θ₁ and the resulting error.
* **The Global Minima:** Our goal is to reach the very bottom of this bowl, where the total error is minimized.
* **The Path:** Gradient descent acts like a ball rolling down from the top of the mountain to the lowest valley.

---

## 2. The Updated Convergence Algorithm

To reach the bottom of the 3D bowl, we must update both θ₀ and θ₁ in an iterative process. The **General Update Rule** remains the same:

**θⱼ := θⱼ - α * (∂ / ∂θⱼ) J(θ₀, θ₁)**

Since we have two parameters, we perform these updates simultaneously:

### Step 1: Updating the Intercept (θ₀)

When we take the partial derivative of the **Mean Squared Error** with respect to θ₀, the math simplifies to finding the average error:

**θ₀ := θ₀ - α * (1/m) * Σ [ hθ(x⁽ⁱ⁾) - y⁽ⁱ⁾ ]**

### Step 2: Updating the Slope (θ₁)

When we take the partial derivative with respect to θ₁, the input feature **x** comes into play (due to the chain rule in calculus):

**θ₁ := θ₁ - α * (1/m) * Σ [ (hθ(x⁽ⁱ⁾) - y⁽ⁱ⁾) * x⁽ⁱ⁾ ]**

---

## 3. Simultaneous Update Logic

It is a "Strict Rule" in gradient descent that you must update θ₀ and θ₁ **simultaneously**. This means:

1. Calculate the gradients for both θ₀ and θ₁ using the **current** weights.
2. Subtract the values at the same time.
3. If you update θ₀ and then use that *new* θ₀ to calculate the update for θ₁, you are no longer following the true gradient of the original surface.

---

## 4. Summary of the Optimized Technique

| Feature | 2D Gradient Descent | 3D Gradient Descent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Parameters** | Only θ₁ (Slope) | θ₀ (Intercept) and θ₁ (Slope) |
| **Visualization** | U-shaped Curve | 3D Bowl / Convex Surface |
| **Intercept** | Fixed at 0 | Variable and Optimized |
| **Goal** | Minimize J by changing Slope | Minimize J by changing both Intercept & Slope |

---

### Final Conclusion on Simple Linear Regression

By repeating these updates until **Convergence**, the algorithm finds the specific values of θ₀ and θ₁ that define the **Best Fit Line**.

* **Low Learning Rate (α):** The "ball" rolls down the mountain slowly but surely.
* **High Learning Rate (α):** The "ball" might bounce across the walls of the bowl and never reach the bottom.
