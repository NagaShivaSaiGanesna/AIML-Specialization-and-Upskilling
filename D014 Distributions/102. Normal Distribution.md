# Normal (Gaussian) Distribution

The **Normal Distribution**, also known as the **Gaussian Distribution**, is perhaps the most important distribution in statistics and data science. Most naturally occurring data—such as human heights, weights, or even errors in measurement—follow this specific pattern.

## 1. Core Concept: The Continuous Curve

Unlike the distributions we discussed previously (Bernoulli, Binomial, Poisson), the Normal distribution is a **Continuous Probability Distribution**.

* **Continuous Data:** It applies to variables that can take any value within a range (e.g., a person's height could be 170.52 cm).
* **The Bell Curve:** The distribution is famous for its characteristic "Bell Curve" shape.

---

## 2. Key Properties

A true Normal distribution has several unique mathematical features:

* **Symmetry:** It is perfectly symmetrical around the center. This means **50%** of the data falls to the left of the center and **50%** falls to the right.
* **The "Center" Rule:** In a perfect Normal distribution, the **Mean**, **Median**, and **Mode** are all equal and located at the exact center of the peak.
* **Parameters:** It is defined by two parameters: **Mean (mu)**, which determines the center, and **Variance (sigma squared)**, which determines the spread (width) of the curve.

---

## 3. Probability Density Function (PDF)

Because the data is continuous, we use a **PDF** to describe the distribution. This function calculates the height of the curve at any given point.

* **mu:** The mean (average) of the data.
* **sigma:** The standard deviation (spread).
* **e and pi:** Mathematical constants.

> **Technical Note:** As the variance (spread) increases, the curve becomes flatter and wider. As variance decreases, the curve becomes taller and narrower.

---

## 4. The Empirical Rule (68-95-99.7 Rule)

One of the most powerful aspects of the Normal distribution is the **Empirical Rule**. This rule allows you to predict exactly where your data points lie based on the **Standard Deviation (sigma)**.

| Range | % of Data Included |
| --- | --- |
| **Mean +/- 1 sigma** | Approximately **68%** |
| **Mean +/- 2 sigma** | Approximately **95%** |
| **Mean +/- 3 sigma** | Approximately **99.7%** |

This rule is vital for your work at **Accordion** because it helps you identify outliers. If a data point falls beyond 3 standard deviations, there is a **99.7%** chance it is an extreme anomaly.

---

## 5. Real-World Examples

* **Human Measurements:** Weights and heights of students in a class.
* **Biology (Iris Data Set):** Features like petal length and sepal width often follow this distribution.
* **Financial Data:** Stock market returns over long periods are often modeled this way.

---

## Summary of Statistics Formulas

| Property | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| **Mean (mu)** | Sum of all values / n |
| **Variance (sigma squared)** | Sum of (value - mean) squared / n |
| **Standard Deviation (sigma)** | Square root of Variance |
