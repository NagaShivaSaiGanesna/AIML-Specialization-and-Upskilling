Exactly. If PMF is the **map** and PDF is the **curve**, the **CDF (Cumulative Distribution Function)** is the **running total**.

It works for both discrete and continuous data. It is the most "practical" tool for a Data Engineer because it answers the question: *"What is the probability that my variable is **less than or equal to** a certain value?"*

---

## Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)

### The "Feel" in 2 Lines

**The CDF is an "accumulator" that adds up all the probabilities from the lowest possible value up to a point .**

**It always starts at 0 (nothing has happened yet) and ends at 1 (100% of all possibilities have been covered).**

---

## The Core Concept: The "Running Total"

Imagine you are playing a video game and collecting coins.

* The **PMF** tells you how many coins are in **each specific room**.
* The **CDF** tells you the **total number of coins** you have collected *up to* the room you are currently in.

---

## Real-World Example: Database Query Latency

Let’s go back to your work at **Accordion**. You want to know if your database is meeting its **SLA (Service Level Agreement)**. The SLA says 95% of queries must finish in under 500ms.

| Time () | CDF Value  | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **100ms** | **0.40** | 40% of queries take 100ms or less. |
| **300ms** | **0.85** | 85% of queries take 300ms or less. |
| **500ms** | **0.96** | 96% of queries take 500ms or less. |
| **1000ms** | **1.00** | 100% of queries take 1000ms or less. |

### Why this is a CDF:

1. **Always Increasing:** The value never goes down. As you move to the right (more time), you can only collect *more* probability, never less.
2. **The Goal:** If you see that at 500ms the CDF is **0.96**, you know you are beating your 95% SLA. You don't care about the *exact* millisecond; you care about the *threshold*.

## Visualizing the Difference (The "Vibe" Check)

| Function | Question it Answers | Visual Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PMF/PDF** | "How likely is this **exact** spot?" | Bars (PMF) or a Bell Curve (PDF) |
| **CDF** | "How likely is this spot **or anything smaller**?" | An **S-shaped curve** (it always goes up) |

---

## Why Data Engineers Love CDFs

In your career as an MLE/Data Engineer, you will use CDFs for **Outlier Detection**.

If you look at the CDF of your data and see that a value falls at **0.999** on the curve, it means 99.9% of all other data is smaller than this point. That is a massive signal that the data point is an **outlier** or a **potential error** in your pipeline.
