# Advanced Regularization: Lasso and Elastic Net Regression

In the previous section, we explored how **Ridge Regression (L2)** prevents overfitting by shrinking coefficients. Now, we move to **Lasso Regression (L1)** and **Elastic Net**, which take model optimization and feature selection to the next level.

---

## 1. Lasso Regression: The L1 Regularization

**Lasso** (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator) is similar to Ridge, but with one critical difference: it can be used for **Feature Selection**.

### The Cost Function

The Lasso cost function adds a penalty term based on the **absolute magnitude** of the slopes:

$$J(\theta) = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{i=1}^{m} (h_{\theta}(x_i) - y_i)^2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{n} |\theta_j|$$

* **Penalty Type:** Absolute value (L1 norm).
* **Key Behavior:** Unlike Ridge, which only shrinks coefficients, Lasso can shrink them all the way to **exactly zero**.

---

## 2. Feature Selection via Lasso

Why is shrinking a coefficient to zero important? Because if the coefficient of a feature is $0$, that feature is effectively **removed** from the model.

### The Relationship Between $\lambda$ and Slope

As you increase the hyperparameter $\lambda$, the global minima of your gradient descent shifts.

* **Small $\lambda$:** Slopes are reduced slightly.
* **Large $\lambda$:** Slopes reach the axis and become **zero**.

### Practical Example

Consider a model with four features:


$$y = \theta_0 + 0.65x_1 + 0.72x_2 + 0.034x_3 + 0.12x_4$$

In this scenario, $x_3$ and $x_4$ have very low coefficients, meaning they aren't strongly correlated with the output. After applying Lasso, the equation might look like this:


$$y = \theta_0 + 0.51x_1 + 0.58x_2 + 0 + 0$$

Lasso identified that $x_3$ and $x_4$ were not important and **removed** them. This makes Lasso ideal for datasets with hundreds of features where you only want to keep the most impactful ones.

---

## 3. Elastic Net Regression: The Hybrid Approach

What if your model is overfitting **and** you have too many features? Instead of choosing between Ridge and Lasso, you use **Elastic Net**.

**Elastic Net** is a combination of both L1 and L2 regularization. It handles the limitations of both by adding two penalty terms.

### The Elastic Net Cost Function

$$J(\theta) = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{i=1}^{m} (h_{\theta}(x_i) - y_i)^2 + \lambda_1 \sum_{j=1}^{n} \theta_j^2 + \lambda_2 \sum_{j=1}^{n} |\theta_j|$$

| Penalty Term | Component | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **$\lambda_1 \sum \theta_j^2$** | Ridge (L2) | Reduces Overfitting (Shrinkage) |

---

## Summary Comparison

| Metric | Ridge (L2) | Lasso (L1) | Elastic Net |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Penalty Term** | Square of Slopes | Absolute of Slopes | Both L1 and L2 |
| **Feature Selection** | No (Coefficients $\neq 0$) | **Yes** (Coefficients $= 0$) | **Yes** |
| **Overfitting** | Excellent at reducing it | Good at reducing it | Best for complex cases |
| **When to use?** | When all features are useful | When many features are useless | When you have a massive feature set |

---

## Conclusion: Hyperparameter Tuning

Ridge, Lasso, and Elastic Net are essentially ways to **hyperparameter tune** your Linear Regression. By adjusting $\lambda$ (or $\alpha$ in some libraries), you control the complexity of your model to find the "sweet spot" between bias and variance.
