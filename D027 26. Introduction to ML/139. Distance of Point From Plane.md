# Distance of a Point from a Hyperplane

Calculating the distance of a point from a hyperplane is a fundamental operation in machine learning, particularly for **Logistic Regression** and **Support Vector Machines (SVM)**. These algorithms use this distance to determine how confidently a data point belongs to a specific class.

---

## 1. The Distance Formula

Given a hyperplane $\pi$ defined by the weight vector $w$ (which is perpendicular to the plane) and an intercept $b$, we can find the distance $d$ from any point $s$ to the plane.

If the plane passes through the origin ($b = 0$), the shortest (perpendicular) distance $d$ is given by:

$$d = \frac{w^{T}s}{||w||}$$

Where:

* $w^{T}s$ is the dot product of the weight vector and the point's coordinates.
* $||w||$ is the **magnitude** (Euclidean norm) of the weight vector, used to normalize the distance.

---

## 2. Geometric Interpretation: Positive vs. Negative Distance

In geometry, distance is usually absolute (always positive). However, in machine learning, we use **signed distance** to understand which side of the "decision boundary" a point falls on.

### Points Above the Plane (Positive Side)

When a point $s$ lies on the same side of the plane that the vector $w$ is pointing toward:

* The angle $\theta$ between vector $w$ and vector $s$ is **acute** ($0^{\circ} \leq \theta < 90^{\circ}$).
* Since $\cos(\theta)$ is positive for acute angles, the result of $w^{T}s$ will be **positive**.
* **Conclusion:** Points in the direction of $w$ have a positive distance.

### Points Below the Plane (Negative Side)

When a point $s'$ lies on the opposite side of the plane:

* The angle $\theta$ between vector $w$ and vector $s'$ is **obtuse** ($\theta > 90^{\circ}$).
* Since $\cos(\theta)$ is negative for obtuse angles, the result of $w^{T}s'$ will be **negative**.
* **Conclusion:** Points in the opposite direction of $w$ have a negative distance.

---

## 3. Why This Matters in Classification

This signed distance is the "secret sauce" for algorithms like SVM and Logistic Regression:

| Sign of Distance | Prediction | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Positive ($d > 0$)** | Class A (+1) | The point is on the "correct" side of the boundary. |
| **Negative ($d < 0$)** | Class B (-1) | The point is on the "other" side of the boundary. |
| **Zero ($d = 0$)** | Boundary | The point lies exactly on the decision plane. |

> **Technical Note:** In classification, we often say that the further the distance (the larger the absolute value of $d$), the higher the **confidence** of the model in its prediction.
