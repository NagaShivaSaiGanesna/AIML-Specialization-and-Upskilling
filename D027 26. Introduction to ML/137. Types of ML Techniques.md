# Introduction to Machine Learning Techniques

Machine Learning (ML) is broadly categorized into three primary methodologies, each designed to address specific types of problem statements. Understanding these categories is the first step in determining which algorithm to apply to a given dataset.

---

## 1. Supervised Machine Learning

**Supervised Machine Learning** is the most common technique used in the industry. It is characterized by the presence of a **labeled dataset**, meaning the data includes both the input information and the desired output.

### Core Concepts

* **Independent Features:** These are the input variables (often denoted as **X**) used to make predictions.
* **Dependent Feature:** Also known as the **Target** or **Output feature** (denoted as **y**), this is the variable we aim to predict. It is "dependent" because its value relies on the changes in the independent features.

### Types of Supervised Learning

Supervised learning is divided based on the nature of the dependent feature:

| Feature | Regression | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Output Type** | Continuous / Numerical | Categorical / Discrete |
| **Goal** | Predict a specific quantity | Assign a label or category |
| **Example** | Predicting house prices ($450k, $500k) | Predicting Pass/Fail or Spam/Not Spam |

#### Regression

In a **Regression** problem, the output is a continuous value. For example, if you are predicting house prices based on the **size (sq ft)** and **number of rooms**, the price can be any value within a range.

#### Classification

In a **Classification** problem, the output consists of distinct categories.

* **Binary Classification:** Only two possible outcomes (e.g., Pass vs. Fail).
* **Multi-class Classification:** More than two categories (e.g., classifying an image as a Cat, Dog, or Bird).

---

## 2. Unsupervised Machine Learning

**Unsupervised Machine Learning** deals with data that does not have a predefined label or output feature. The goal is not to predict a value, but to discover hidden patterns or structures within the data.

### Customer Segmentation & Clustering

The most common application is **Clustering**. Imagine an e-commerce company analyzing customer data based on **Salary** and **Spending Score**. Since there is no "correct answer" provided in the data, the algorithm groups customers with similar behaviors into **Clusters**.

* **Cluster A:** High Salary, High Spending (Premium targets for discounts).
* **Cluster B:** Low Salary, Low Spending (Frugal group).
* **Cluster C:** High Salary, Low Spending (Potential savers).

By identifying these groups, businesses can tailor their marketing strategies without needing a historical "label" for every customer.

---

## 3. Reinforcement Learning (RL)

**Reinforcement Learning** is a reward-based learning system. Unlike the previous two methods, RL learns through trial and error by interacting with an environment.

### The Feedback Loop

Conceptually, RL mimics how humans learn. Think of a toddler learning to walk:

1. **Action:** The child attempts a step.
2. **Penalty:** If they fall, they experience "pain" (a negative reward).
3. **Reward:** If they succeed, they reach their destination or receive praise (a positive reward).
4. **Optimization:** Over time, the "agent" (the child or the software) adjusts its actions to maximize positive rewards and minimize negative ones.
