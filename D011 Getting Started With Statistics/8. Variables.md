# Understanding Variables in Statistics

In statistics, data is categorized based on how it is measured and recorded. To manage this data effectively, we use **Variables**.

## 1. What is a Variable?

A **Variable** is a specific property or characteristic that can take on different values.

* **Key Rule**: A variable represents a single property at a time (e.g., "Age = 25").
* **Contrast**: A list of ages (12, 20, 25) is a **dataset** or a **collection**, not a single variable instance.

---

## 2. Types of Variables

Variables are broadly classified into two main categories: **Quantitative** (Numerical) and **Qualitative** (Categorical).

### A. Quantitative Variables

These are variables that represent measurable quantities and are expressed in numbers. They answer the question "How much?" or "How many?".

#### i. Discrete Quantitative Variables

These variables consist of **whole numbers** (integers). They represent items that can be counted but not divided into fractions.

* **Examples**:
* **Number of children**: You can have 2 or 3 children, but never 2.5.
* **Bank accounts**: You can own 1 or 5 accounts, but not 5.5.
* **Number of students**: A classroom has 50 students, not 50.75.



#### ii. Continuous Quantitative Variables

These variables can take on **any value within a range**, including decimals and fractions. They are usually the result of measurements.

* **Examples**:
* **Height**: Someone can be 175.5 cm tall.
* **Weight**: A person might weigh 72.7 kg.
* **Temperature**: The weather can be 98.6 degrees.



### B. Qualitative (Categorical) Variables

These variables represent characteristics or "qualities" that cannot be measured numerically. Instead, they describe groups or categories.

* **Examples**:
* **Gender**: Male, Female, Non-binary.
* **Colors**: Red, Green, Blue.
* **States/Places**: Telangana, California, London.



---

## 3. Comparison Summary

| Feature | Discrete (Quantitative) | Continuous (Quantitative) | Qualitative (Categorical) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Data Type** | Whole Numbers | Decimals/Fractions | Text/Labels |
| **Method** | Counting | Measuring | Classifying |
| **Example** | Number of cars | Precise height | Car model name |

---

### Interview Tip: Common Confusion

A common interview question asks whether **Age** is discrete or continuous.

* **Strictly speaking**: Age is **continuous** because time is continuous (you are 25.456... years old).
* **In practice**: It is often treated as **discrete** because we usually report it in whole years.

Always clarify the context!
