# Measures of Central Tendency

In statistics, **Measures of Central Tendency** are descriptive summary measures that help us identify the center point or "typical" value of a dataset. These measures provide a single value that represents the entire distribution, allowing us to understand where most data points reside.

The three primary measures are **Mean**, **Median**, and **Mode**.

---

## 1. Mean (Average)

The Mean is the most common measure of central tendency. It is calculated by summing all observations in a dataset and dividing by the total number of observations. However, the notation changes depending on whether we are analyzing a **Population** or a **Sample**.

### Mathematical Formulas

| Entity | Notation | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Population Mean** | mu | (sum of x) / N |
| **Sample Mean** | x-bar | (sum of x) / n |

* **N**: Total number of individuals in the population.
* **n**: Total number of individuals in the sample.
* **x**: Individual data points.

### Calculation Example

If we have a dataset of ages: **1, 3, 4, 5**

* **Sum**: 1 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 13
* **Count**: 4
* **Mean**: 13 / 4 = **3.25**

---

## 2. Median

The Median is the **middle value** of a dataset when the observations are arranged in order. Unlike the mean, the median is highly **robust to outliers** (extreme values that differ significantly from the rest of the data).

### Steps to Calculate the Median

1. **Sort the Data**: Arrange the data in ascending or descending order.
2. **Identify the Middle**:
* **If n is Odd**: The median is the exact middle element.
* **If n is Even**: The median is the average of the two central elements.



### The Impact of Outliers

Consider the previous age dataset **(1, 3, 4, 5)** with an added outlier of **100**.

* **Sorted Data**: 1, 3, 4, 5, 100
* **Mean Calculation**: (1 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 100) / 5 = **22.6**
* **Median Calculation**: The middle element is **4**.

**Observation**: The mean jumped from 3.25 to 22.6 because of one large number, which misrepresents the "typical" age. The median only moved from 3.5 to 4, providing a much more accurate center for the majority of the group.

---

## 3. Mode

The Mode is the value that appears with the **highest frequency** in a dataset.

* **Use Case**: Mode is particularly useful for categorical data or when you want to identify the most popular or frequent occurrence.
* **Outlier Resistance**: Like the median, the mode is not affected by outliers because it only cares about how often a specific value repeats, not the magnitude of the values.

### Example

Dataset: **1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 100**

* **Frequency of 1**: 2
* **Frequency of 2**: 2
* **Frequency of 4**: 3
* **Mode**: **4** (Since it appears most frequently).

---

## Summary Comparison

| Measure | Definition | Sensitivity to Outliers | Best Used For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mean** | Mathematical average | **High** (Easily skewed) | Symmetric data with no outliers |
| **Median** | Middle value | **Low** (Robust) | Skewed data or datasets with outliers |
| **Mode** | Most frequent value | **Lowest** | Categorical data or finding the most common case |
