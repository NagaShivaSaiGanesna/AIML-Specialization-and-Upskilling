# Summary of Statistical Formulas: Population vs. Sample

This guide provides a comprehensive review of the formulas used in both descriptive and inferential statistics. It is critical to distinguish between these two because the notations and denominators change depending on whether you are analyzing a complete population or a representative sample.

---

## 1. Population Parameters

Population data represents the entire group you are studying. We use Greek letters to represent these parameters.

| Concept | Symbol | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Population Size** | **N** | Total count of individuals in the population |
| **Population Mean** | **mu** | sum of x / N |
| **Population Variance** | **sigma^2** | sum(x - mu)^2 / N |
| **Population Standard Deviation** | **sigma** | sqrt(sigma^2) |

---

## 2. Sample Statistics

Sample data is a subset of the population. We use standard English letters for these statistics.

| Concept | Symbol | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sample Size** | **n** | Total count of individuals in the sample |
| **Sample Mean** | **x-bar** | sum of x / n |
| **Sample Variance** | **s^2** | sum(x - x-bar)^2 / (n - 1) |
| **Sample Standard Deviation** | **s** | sqrt(s^2) |

> **Note on Bessel's Correction:** Remember that we use **n - 1** for sample variance to ensure an unbiased estimate of the population variance.

---

## 3. Understanding Standard Deviation

While **Variance** gives us a mathematical sense of spread, it is in "squared units" (e.g., if data is in "years," variance is in "years squared"). **Standard Deviation** brings the units back to the original scale by taking the square root.

### The Purpose

Standard Deviation tells us **how far a specific data point is from the mean** in standardized units.

* **Example:** If the Mean (mu) = 3 and the Standard Deviation (sigma) = 1:
* A value of **4** is exactly **1 standard deviation** to the right of the mean.
* A value of **2** is exactly **1 standard deviation** to the left of the mean.
* A value of **4.5** is exactly **1.5 standard deviations** away from the mean.



---

## 4. Key Comparative Tools

### Population vs. Sample Reference Table

| Feature | Population | Sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Goal** | Describe the entire group | Infer characteristics of the population |
| **Mean Notation** | mu | x-bar |
| **Variance Notation** | sigma^2 | s^2 |
| **Denominator** | N | n - 1 (Bessel's Correction) |
| **Standard Deviation** | sigma | s |

### Checklist for Scannability

* **Mean (Central Tendency):** The mathematical average.
* **Variance (Dispersion):** The average squared distance from the mean.
* **Standard Deviation:** The square root of variance; indicates "standard" distance from the center.
* **n - 1:** Used specifically for sample calculations to prevent underestimation of spread.
