# Bessel's Correction: Why Divide by n - 1?

In our previous discussion, we noted that the formula for **Sample Variance (s^2)** uses **n - 1** in the denominator, while **Population Variance (sigma^2)** uses **N**. This adjustment is known as **Bessel's Correction**. It is a fundamental concept in statistics and a frequent interview question.

---

## 1. Population vs. Sample Formulas

To understand the "why," let's compare the two notations:

| Measurement | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| **Population Variance (sigma^2)** | sum(x - mu)^2 / **N** |
| **Sample Variance (s^2)** | sum(x - x-bar)^2 / **(n - 1)** |

* **mu**: True population mean.
* **x-bar**: Sample mean (an estimate of mu).
* **n - 1**: The adjusted denominator used to correct bias.

---

## 2. The Concept of Underestimation

The primary goal of calculating sample statistics is to make **inferences** about the population.

### The Random Sample Scenario

When we pick a perfectly random sample, the sample mean (x-bar) and sample variance (s^2) are usually close to the population mean (mu) and population variance (sigma^2).

### The "Tight Cluster" Problem

Imagine a population spread across a wide range. If we happen to pick a sample where the data points are clustered closely together (even if they are far from the true population mean), the calculated variance using **n** will be very small.

In this case:

1. **x-bar** is much smaller (or different) than **mu**.
2. **s^2** (if divided by **n**) would be much smaller than the true **sigma^2**.

If we divide by **n**, we are **underestimating** the true variability of the population. This is known as a **Biased Estimator**.

---

## 3. How Bessel's Correction Fixes Bias

To move from a biased estimate to an **Unbiased Estimation**, we need to increase the result of our variance calculation.

* By changing the denominator from **n** to **n - 1**, we are dividing by a **smaller number**.
* Mathematically, dividing by a smaller number results in a **larger quotient**.
* This "inflates" the sample variance slightly to account for the fact that we might have missed the full spread of the population.

This correction ensures that if we took many different samples and averaged their variances, the result would equal the true population variance.

---

## 4. Degree of Freedom (DOF)

Another way to explain **n - 1** to an interviewer is through the concept of **Degree of Freedom**.

* **Definition**: The number of independent values or quantities which can be assigned to a statistical distribution.
* In a sample of size **n**, once we calculate the sample mean (**x-bar**), only **n - 1** data points are free to vary. The last data point is "fixed" because all points must sum up to a value that results in that specific mean.
* Therefore, we say the sample has **n - 1 degrees of freedom**.

---

### Key Summary for Interviews

> **"We divide by n - 1 because using n in a sample results in a biased estimator that underestimates the population variance. Bessel's Correction (n - 1) compensates for this bias, providing an unbiased estimate of the true population spread."**
