## 1. Linear Regression: The Baseline

Standard **Linear Regression** aims to minimize the **Residual Sum of Squares (RSS)**. It assumes that the relationship between weather features and FWI is strictly additive.

* **R-Squared ($R^2$):** $\approx 98\%$
* **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):** $\approx 0.54$
* **Observation:** While $98\%$ looks amazing, we must be cautious. A model this "perfect" on training/test splits might be **overfitting** to the specific noise of the Algerian dataset.

---

## 2. Lasso Regression (L1): The Feature Selector

**Lasso** adds a penalty equal to the absolute value of the magnitude of coefficients ($L1$ norm).

$$Cost = \text{MSE} + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{n} |\theta_j|$$

* **R-Squared ($R^2$):** $\approx 95\%$
* **MAE:** $\approx 1.13$
* **The "Lasso Effect":** Notice the $R^2$ dropped slightly. This is actually a good sign! Lasso is likely pushing the coefficients of less important weather features (like maybe a specific wind direction or minor humidity fluctuation) to **zero**. It is simplifying our model to make it more robust.

---

## 3. Ridge Regression (L2): The Overfitting Shield

**Ridge** adds a penalty equal to the square of the magnitude of coefficients ($L2$ norm).

$$Cost = \text{MSE} + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{n} \theta_j^2$$

* **R-Squared ($R^2$):** $\approx 98\%$
* **The "Ridge Effect":** Ridge keeps all features but shrinks their influence. It is excellent at handling the multicollinearity we saw earlier between features like BUI and DC. It provides a stable "Best Fit" without deleting information.

---

## 4. Elastic Net: The Hybrid

**Elastic Net** combines both $L1$ and $L2$ penalties. It is useful when there are multiple correlated features where Lasso might be too aggressive and Ridge might be too passive.

* **R-Squared ($R^2$):** $\approx 87\%$
* **Observation:** The performance dropped significantly here because the default **Alpha** and **L1 Ratio** parameters aren't optimized for our data yet. This leads us directly to the need for **Hyperparameter Tuning**.

## 1. The "What If" Scenarios

### Q1: What if you have 10,000 features but only 200 rows?

**The Answer:** You should **never** use standard Linear Regression.

* **The Reason:** When the number of features ($n$) is much larger than the number of observations ($m$), the $X^TX$ matrix becomes non-invertible. The model will overfit perfectly to the training data (zero error) but fail completely on new data.
* **The Strategy:** Use **Lasso (L1)**. Since you only have 200 rows, only a small number of features can truly be "predictive" without being noise. Lasso will force most of those 10,000 features to zero, effectively performing feature selection and giving you a manageable model.

### Q2: What if your MAE is low, but your $R^2$ is also low?

**The Answer:** This means your model is making small errors, but it is **not explaining the variance** of the target.

* **The Reason:** $R^2$ measures how much better your model is than simply guessing the average (mean) value. If the FWI values are all very close together (low variance), your Mean Absolute Error (MAE) will naturally be small. However, if your model still can't predict the small fluctuations within that range, the $R^2$ will be low. It indicates your features are not strongly "correlated" with the target's movement.

### Q3: What if you want to find the optimal $\lambda$ (Alpha)?

**The Answer:** You use **Cross-Validation versions** of the models (e.g., `LassoCV`, `RidgeCV`).

* **The Reason:** Instead of manually guessing if Alpha should be $0.1$ or $1.0$, these classes automatically split the training data into multiple folds, test a range of Alpha values, and pick the one that results in the lowest error across all folds. This is the most scientifically sound way to regularize your model.

## 2. System Design: The Automated Retraining Worker

### Designing the "Model Champion" Pipeline

To maintain a high-performing model for the Algerian Forest Service, the architecture should look like this:

1. **Candidate Training:** When 500 new points arrive, a worker triggers a training job. It uses **Time-Series Split** (to respect the order of weather data) and finds the best Alpha via `RidgeCV`.
2. **Shadow Testing:** The new "Candidate" and the "Current Champion" are both fed the next hour of real-time data. The Candidate’s predictions are recorded but **not used** to trigger actual fire alerts.
3. **Evaluation Gate:** A service compares the $R^2$ and MAE of both models.
* *Threshold:* `If (Candidate_R2 > Champion_R2 * 1.02) AND (Candidate_MAE <= Champion_MAE)`.


4. **Canary Deployment & Rollback:**
* **Hot-Swap:** If it passes the gate, 10% of traffic is routed to the Candidate.
* **Rollback:** If the error rate on the live 10% spikes, the system automatically kills the Candidate and reverts 100% of traffic back to the Champion.
